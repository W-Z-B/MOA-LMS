# ADR 0006: Academic integrity without detectors or webcams

**Status:** accepted on the recommended answer, 5 October 2026 (decision D4); GSA may revise.
**Date:** 5 October 2026.

## Context

Turnitin is the norm (the University of Guyana uses it), but it is charged per student and sends students'
work abroad, which is a transfer of personal data under the Data Protection Act 2023. Detectors of AI writing
have documented false alarms, and the University of Guyana's own use of Turnitin has drawn public complaint.
Webcam proctoring collects biometric data, which the Act treats as sensitive, and needs bandwidth students do
not have. Some certificate students may be under 18. Practicals, orals and field observation are already a
secure way to assess, and they are central to an agricultural school.

## Decision

1. **Similarity within GSA.** Each submission is compared with other GSA submissions, past and present, on
   GSA's own server. Matching passages are shown to the lecturer only, as evidence for a person to weigh
   (item 3.20).
2. **No detector of AI writing**, and no score that claims to say whether a person or a machine wrote a text.
3. **No webcam proctoring** and no biometric collection.
4. **Two lanes.** Practicals, orals and field observation are the secure lane. Written work states what use
   of AI is allowed, and students declare what they used.
5. An integrity statement and guidance for lecturers accompany the first release that assesses.

## Consequences

- No per-student fee and no transfer of learner work abroad for integrity checks.
- The similarity check finds copying within GSA only, not from the web or other institutions. Lecturers
  are told so in the guidance.
- No decision about a student is made by the check alone; a lecturer decides, and the student can answer.
