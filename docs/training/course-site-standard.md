# GSA course-site standard

**Version 1.0, 6 October 2026, draft for GSA's review.** For: every lecturer who teaches a course in the
LMS, and the department champion who checks the course site with them.

Every course in the pilot has a course site in the LMS that meets this standard **by the end of week 2 of
term**. This is part of checklist item **7.15**, and it is how GSA meets the quality bar "Used": every
course has its outline, weekly content and assessment in the LMS by week two, and its students log in
weekly.

The standard is the least a course site must have. Lecturers are free to add more.

## How to use this checklist

1. The lecturer builds the site, using the "Where in the LMS" column.
2. The lecturer and the champion go through the list together, on a computer and then on a phone.
3. The champion ticks each line that is met and writes the date and both names at the bottom.
4. Anything not met is written under "To finish", with a date. The champion checks it again on that date.

A quick way to start: on the course site, open **Course setup: template, copy from another course, dates
and storage**. Use **Start from a template** for a new course, or **Copy from an earlier course** (with
**Move its dates**) for a course taught before.

## Notes on some lines

- **Line 3, dates.** Students see a module from its date. Set the dates for the whole term at the start;
  **Course setup** has **Move every date** if the term moves.
- **Line 5, licences.** Choose "GSA's own" for material written at GSA. For anything else, name the
  owner and the licence, or the permission. If you are not sure, ask the champion before you upload.
  Material that came in through a folder import is marked "Not yet known" and must be checked.
- **Line 6, weights.** The weights in the LMS must match the weights in the course outline.
- **Line 9, accessibility.** The editor will not save a page with a finding that **Must be fixed**. Fix
  the others too where you can.
- **Line 14, phones.** Most students use a phone, often with weak signal. Prefer short pages to large
  files. A PDF scan of a whole book is hard to read on a phone and slow to download.
