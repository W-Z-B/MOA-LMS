# Department champion: role description

**Version 1.0, 6 October 2026, draft for GSA's review.** For: heads of department, who choose the
champions; the champions themselves; and the course administrators, who support them.

Each department has one LMS champion. This is part of checklist item **7.15**: lecturer training in small
groups by department, a GSA course-site standard, and a champion in each department.

## Purpose

The champion is the first person a lecturer in the department asks about the LMS. They help colleagues
get their course sites to the [course-site standard](course-site-standard.md) by week 2 of term, and they
tell the course administrators what is not working. A question answered down the corridor is answered
faster than one sent across the campus.

## Who

- A lecturer or instructor in the department who teaches at least one course in the pilot.
- Comfortable with a computer and a phone, and patient with colleagues. They do not need to be an expert.
- Willing to learn the LMS a little ahead of everyone else.
- In a large department, or one that teaches on both campuses (Mon Repos and Essequibo), there may be a
  second champion.

## Time

| Period | About how much time |
|---|---|
| Before term (training and preparation) | The three training sessions, plus an early briefing of about 2 hours |
| The pilot term | **About 2 hours a week**, most of it in weeks 1 to 4 |
| After the pilot term | About 1 hour a week, less once the department is settled |

The head of department agrees this time with the champion and allows for it in their teaching load.

## Duties

1. **Learn early.** Attend the early briefing and try new parts of the LMS before colleagues do.
2. **Help at training.** Attend the department's three sessions and help anyone who falls behind.
3. **Check course sites.** Go through the course-site standard with each lecturer in the department, tick
   the checklist, and agree a date for anything left to finish.
4. **Report at week 2.** Send the department's ticked checklists to the course administrators at the end
   of week 2 of term.
5. **Answer first questions.** Be the first person colleagues ask. Show them the **Help** link and how to
   use **Ask for help**.
6. **Watch students' first weeks.** With the lecturers, look at **Not seen in N days** on the Home page,
   and encourage lecturers to follow up students who have not logged in.
7. **Take a turn at the clinic.** Help at the weekly clinic on a rota with the other champions.
8. **Pass on what is wrong.** Tell the course administrators about anything that does not work, is hard
   to use, or is wrong in the quick guides or help pages.
9. **Meet the other champions.** A short meeting of all champions with the course administrators every
   two weeks in the pilot term, monthly after.

The champion does **not** build colleagues' course sites for them, mark their work, or hold their
passwords. Each lecturer keeps their own password and authenticator code.

## What the champion gets

- **Early access**: new parts of the LMS on the training system before they reach everyone else.
- **A direct line to the course administrators** for the department's questions, answered first.
- The early briefing and a place in every department's training, if they wish.
- A named role, recorded in their staff file, that counts towards their own development.

## How the champion escalates

| The problem | What the champion does |
|---|---|
| A colleague cannot do something the quick guide covers | Shows them, or brings them to the clinic |
| Something seems wrong on the screen, or a question the champion cannot answer | **Ask for help** from that page (it sends the page with the question), or the direct line to the course administrators |
| A colleague cannot sign in, or has lost the phone with their authenticator | Sends them to the course administrators at once. The champion never resets anyone's sign-in |
| A student's marks, records or personal data look wrong | To the course administrators, who decide whether it goes to the data protection officer |
| A course site will not meet the standard by week 2 | Tells the head of department and the course administrators, with the reason and a new date |
| Something that might be a data breach (for example, a student seeing another student's work) | Tells the course administrators the same day. They record it in the breach register |

## How the champion is chosen

1. The course administrators explain the role to the heads of department before training begins.
2. Each head of department asks for a volunteer and, with them, names one champion (or two, as above).
3. The head of department tells the course administrators the name before the department's first session.
4. The role is for one academic year, and can be renewed. If a champion leaves or steps down, the head of
   department names another.

## How the champion is recognised

- Thanked by name by the Principal at the end of the pilot term, with the department's week 2 results.
- A letter from the Principal for their staff file, describing the role and the time given.
- The role counts in their annual appraisal, as service to the School.
- Where the LMS offers a staff-development course for champions, its certificate is issued to them.
