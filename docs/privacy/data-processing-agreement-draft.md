# Data processing agreement: GSA LMS

**Version 0.1 draft, 6 October 2026.** Checklist item 7.21 (gap G3).

> **DRAFT FOR GSA'S LEGAL ADVISER. THIS IS NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** The development team prepared it as a
> starting point, from the impact assessments of the LMS and the HRMS. It has not been reviewed by a
> lawyer. The Data Protection Act No. 18 of 2023 received assent on 16 August 2023; as far as press reports
> say, no commencement order had been issued by September 2026. The points below follow the published
> summary of the Act used for the impact assessments, which does not give section numbers. Every reference
> written **[s. __]** is for the legal adviser to fill in, and every item in square brackets is for GSA or
> the adviser to complete or confirm.

**Read with:** the LMS impact assessment (`docs/privacy/dpia.md`), what the LMS records
(`docs/privacy/what-we-record.md`), the privacy notice draft (`docs/privacy/privacy-notice-draft.md`), and the
HRMS impact assessment (`HRMS/gsa-hrms/docs/privacy/dpia.md`, section 3 and question 2 of section 8).

**One agreement for the three systems.** If the same provider hosts, runs and supports the HRMS and the SRMS
as well as the LMS, GSA should sign this agreement once for all three, with a set of schedules for each
system (its own processing details, security measures, sub-processors and retention periods). The clauses
are the same for all three; only the schedules differ. This keeps one set of promises, one breach procedure
and one end-of-contract procedure, and means GSA's legal adviser reviews one text instead of three. The
schedules below are those for the LMS; the HRMS's are drawn from its own impact assessment.

## Background

A. GSA runs the LMS for teaching and learning, and decides why and how personal data in it is processed.
B. The Provider hosts, runs and supports the LMS for GSA under [the services contract dated ____] (the
   "Services Contract").
C. The Act requires a controller that uses a processor to bind it by a written contract stating the
   subject-matter and duration of the processing, its nature and purpose, the types of personal data and
   categories of people, and the controller's obligations and rights **[s. __]**. It also requires
   processors to be registered **[s. __]**. This agreement is that contract.

## 1. Meaning of words

1.1 "The Act" means the Data Protection Act No. 18 of 2023 and any regulations made under it.

1.2 "Personal data", "processing", "controller", "processor", "data subject", "sensitive personal data"
and "personal data breach" have the meanings the Act gives them **[s. __]**. In plain words, a "data
subject" is the person the data is about: a student, a member of staff or anyone else with an account.

1.3 "Commissioner" means the Data Protection Commissioner under the Act.

1.4 "Instructions" means this agreement, the Services Contract, and any further written instruction from
GSA's authorised contacts in Schedule D.

1.5 "Sub-processor" means anyone the Provider engages to process GSA's personal data.

1.6 "GSA Data" means the personal data the Provider processes for GSA under the Services Contract.

## 2. Subject-matter and duration

2.1 The Provider processes GSA Data only to host, run, maintain, back up, restore and support the LMS, as
the Services Contract describes.

2.2 This agreement starts on the date of the last signature and lasts as long as the Provider processes
any GSA Data, including after the Services Contract ends, until the data is deleted or returned under
clause 13.

## 3. Nature and purpose of the processing

3.1 **Nature:** storing, backing up, restoring, transmitting, organising and, where support needs it,
viewing GSA Data on GSA's behalf. The Provider does not decide what is collected, why, or for how long.

3.2 **Purpose:** to let GSA run its courses, assess students' work, keep records of learning, keep the
system secure, and meet its duties under the Act. Schedule A sets out the detail.

3.3 The Provider must not process GSA Data for any purpose of its own, including marketing, analytics,
product development or training any automated or artificial intelligence system.

## 4. Types of personal data and categories of people

4.1 The categories of people are:

- **students**, including students who may be **under 18** (school leavers on certificate and diploma
  programmes);
- **staff**: lecturers, teaching assistants, course administrators and other staff with an account;
- **everyone with an account**, including the Data Protection Officer and the auditor.

4.2 The types of personal data are listed in Schedule A. They include work students hand in and the marks
and feedback they receive, which decide progression and can affect a person's future. The LMS holds no
date of birth, national identifier, health record or bank detail.

4.3 Because some students are under 18, the Provider treats all student data with the care due to
children's data. GSA's legal adviser to confirm what the Act requires for children's data **[s. __]**.

## 5. GSA's obligations and rights

5.1 GSA as controller:

(a) decides the purposes and means of the processing, and is responsible for having a lawful basis for it;
(b) gives its Instructions in writing, through the contacts in Schedule D;
(c) tells people what is processed and why, through the LMS privacy notice;
(d) answers requests from data subjects, with the Provider's help under clause 9;
(e) decides whether a breach must be reported to the Commissioner and to the people affected, and does so;
(f) keeps the record of processing activities the Act requires of a controller **[s. __]**, with the
    Provider's help under clause 15;
(g) confirms the retention periods in Schedule E;
(h) registers as a controller when the Act requires it **[s. __]**.

5.2 GSA has the right to:

(a) give, change and withdraw Instructions;
(b) receive the information it needs to show that this agreement and the Act are being followed;
(c) audit and inspect under clause 14;
(d) approve or refuse sub-processors under clause 8, and transfers under clause 16;
(e) have GSA Data returned or deleted at any time, and at the end under clause 13;
(f) suspend the processing, or end this agreement, under clause 18 if the Provider breaks it.

## 6. Acting only on GSA's instructions

6.1 The Provider processes GSA Data only on GSA's documented Instructions, including on transfers outside
Guyana, unless a law of Guyana requires otherwise. In that case the Provider tells GSA before processing,
unless that law forbids telling.

6.2 If the Provider thinks an Instruction breaks the Act, it tells GSA at once and need not follow it until
GSA confirms or changes it in writing.

6.3 The Provider does not change how the LMS processes personal data (for example by recording something new
about what people do) except through a change GSA has approved. A change that records something new must
first be added to `what-we-record.md`, the privacy notice, the retention schedule and the impact assessment,
as that page requires.

## 7. Confidentiality of staff

7.1 The Provider allows only those of its staff who need GSA Data for the services to have access to it, and
only to the extent they need.

7.2 Each such person is bound by a written duty of confidentiality that continues after their work for the
Provider ends, and is trained in data protection and in the security measures of Schedule B before being
given access.

7.3 The Provider keeps a list of the people with access to the production system and its backups, and gives
it to GSA on request and at each access review (each term).

## 8. Sub-processors

8.1 The Provider does not engage a sub-processor without GSA's **prior written approval** of that
particular sub-processor. Approved sub-processors are listed in Schedule C.

8.2 The Provider asks for approval at least [30] days before the sub-processor would start, naming it, its
location, what it would do and which GSA Data it would see.

8.3 The Provider binds each sub-processor by a written contract with the same obligations as this agreement,
and remains fully responsible to GSA for what the sub-processor does.

8.4 A sub-processor is engaged only if the data stays in Guyana, or GSA has approved a transfer under
clause 16.

## 9. Help with people's rights

9.1 The LMS provides most of what GSA needs to answer requests from data subjects, and the Provider keeps
these working:

(a) **access:** "My data", where each person reads and downloads their whole record, every viewing and
    download being audited; an administrator or the DPO produces the same record for a request made on paper,
    including one from a parent or guardian through the Registry;
(b) **correction:** correction requests made from My data and answered by a course administrator within
    `PRIVACY_RESPONSE_DAYS` (30 days); names, numbers and class lists are corrected in the SRMS or HRMS;
(c) **retention and disposal:** the retention schedule held as data in the system, disposal approved by a
    second person, each destruction audited, and logs removed every night.

9.2 Where a request cannot be answered through the system, the Provider helps GSA within [5] working days of
being asked, at no extra charge [GSA to confirm].

9.3 If a data subject contacts the Provider directly, the Provider passes the request to GSA's DPO within
[2] working days and does not answer it itself unless GSA asks it to.

9.4 Restriction and objection are not built in the LMS today (the HRMS has them, its item 1.46). If GSA
decides learners need them, the Provider helps build or apply them as GSA instructs.

## 10. Security

10.1 The Provider keeps in place the technical and organisational measures in Schedule B, which reflect what
the LMS does today, and keeps them at least as strong for the life of this agreement.

10.2 The Provider does not weaken a measure in Schedule B without GSA's written approval. It tells GSA of any
weakness it finds, and of how and when it will be fixed.

10.3 Staging and test systems hold **fictional data only**. Real GSA Data is never copied to a system outside
production and its backups, including for fault-finding, without GSA's written approval for that occasion.

## 11. Personal data breaches

11.1 The Provider tells GSA's DPO and the Principal of any personal data breach affecting GSA Data, or any
reasonable suspicion of one, **without undue delay and in any case within 24 hours** of becoming aware of it.
[24 hours is proposed so that GSA can meet its own duty to tell the Commissioner, which the summary of the Act
puts at 72 hours **[s. __]**; GSA's legal adviser to confirm both periods.]

11.2 The notice gives, as far as known at the time, and the rest as soon as it is known, the fields of the
LMS breach register:

- when the breach was found, and when it began if known;
- what happened;
- whose data, and which types of data;
- how many people are affected, and **whether students under 18 are among them**;
- the likely risk to the people affected;
- when, and how, it was contained;
- what has been done, and what is proposed, to deal with it and limit its harm;
- a contact at the Provider for further information.

11.3 GSA records the breach in the LMS breach register, which alerts the administrators and the DPO; a
breach is closed only once it is contained. GSA decides whether, when and how to tell the Commissioner and the
people affected. The Provider does not tell them itself unless GSA asks it to in writing or the law requires
it.

11.4 The Provider helps GSA investigate, contain and recover from the breach, keeps the evidence, including
the audit log, and does not delete anything relevant until GSA agrees.

## 12. Impact assessments and the Commissioner

12.1 The Provider helps GSA prepare and keep up to date its data protection impact assessments for the LMS
(and, where clause 18.4 applies, for the HRMS and SRMS), by giving information about the hosting, the security
measures, sub-processors and any change it proposes.

12.2 If GSA consults the Commissioner, or the Commissioner asks questions or inspects, the Provider gives GSA
the information and help it reasonably needs **[s. __]**.

## 13. Deletion or return of data at the end

13.1 When the Services Contract ends, or earlier if GSA asks, the Provider, as GSA chooses in writing:

(a) returns all GSA Data to GSA in a usable form (a full database dump and a copy of the file store, with the
    instructions to restore them), and then deletes it; or
(b) deletes it without returning it.

13.2 Deletion covers every copy, including backups and the off-site copy, within [30] days of GSA's choice,
or, for backups that cannot be deleted sooner, as soon as their normal cycle ends, which must be within [90]
days. Until then backups stay encrypted and are not restored except at GSA's request.

13.3 The Provider gives GSA a **signed certificate of deletion**, naming what was deleted, from where, how and
when, and confirming that no copy remains with the Provider or any sub-processor.

13.4 The Provider keeps GSA Data after the end only where a law of Guyana requires it, tells GSA what it keeps
and why, and keeps it under this agreement until it is deleted.

## 14. Audits and inspections

14.1 The Provider makes available to GSA all information needed to show that this agreement is being kept,
and allows and contributes to audits and inspections by GSA, its auditor or another auditor GSA appoints.

14.2 GSA gives at least [10] working days' notice of an audit, except after a breach or where the
Commissioner requires it, and the auditor is bound by confidentiality.

14.3 The LMS supports this directly: the auditor role reads the chained audit log and its export, the nightly
check of the chain, and the access review signed off each term.

## 15. Records of processing

15.1 The Provider keeps a written record of the processing it carries out for GSA: GSA's name and DPO, the
Provider's name and contact, the categories of processing, any transfers outside Guyana, and a general
description of the security measures **[s. __; the adviser to confirm whether the Act requires a processor to
keep one]**.

15.2 The Provider gives GSA a copy on request, and helps GSA keep its own record of processing activities (the
starting point for which is section 3 of the LMS impact assessment).

## 16. Transfers outside Guyana

16.1 Real GSA Data stays in Guyana. The Provider does not transfer it, or allow it to be reached from, outside
Guyana without GSA's prior written approval, and then only with the safeguards the Act requires **[s. __]**.

16.2 No transfer is planned. Two points are still to be confirmed and, if either involves a transfer, approved
in writing and listed in Schedule C:

(a) **email:** notifications are emailed; where GSA's email service is hosted;
(b) **off-site backups:** where the off-site copy of the daily backup is kept (item 7.09).

16.3 Staging on Railway (United States) holds fictional data only and so involves no transfer of GSA Data.

## 17. Liability and insurance

*Points for the legal adviser; no text is proposed.*

17.1 Whether the Provider's liability for a breach of this agreement or of the Act is capped, and if so at
what level, and whether the cap applies to data protection breaches at all.

17.2 An indemnity from the Provider for fines, claims and costs caused by its breach of this agreement.

17.3 Professional indemnity and cyber insurance the Provider must hold, the amount, and proof each year.

17.4 How liability is shared with a sub-processor engaged by the Provider.

## 18. Term and termination

18.1 This agreement lasts as clause 2.2 says.

18.2 GSA may suspend the processing, or end this agreement and the Services Contract, by written notice if the
Provider seriously or repeatedly breaks this agreement, fails to put a breach right within [30] days of
notice, or is no longer registered as a processor where the Act requires it **[s. __]**.

18.3 Clauses 7 (confidentiality), 11 (breaches), 13 (deletion or return), 14 (audits) and 17 (liability)
continue after this agreement ends, for as long as is needed.

18.4 **Other GSA systems.** Where the Provider also hosts, runs or supports the HRMS or the SRMS, this agreement
covers them too, each with its own Schedules A to E attached and signed. A new system is added by signing its
schedules.

## 19. General

19.1 If this agreement and the Services Contract disagree on data protection, this agreement wins.

19.2 A change to this agreement or its schedules is valid only in writing, signed by both parties.

19.3 Notices go to the contacts in Schedule D.

19.4 If the Act commences, or regulations are made, in terms that require changes to this agreement, the
parties agree in good faith the changes needed.

## 20. Governing law

This agreement is governed by the laws of Guyana, and the courts of Guyana have jurisdiction over any dispute
under it.

## Schedule B: security measures (LMS)

What the LMS does today, and what the Provider operates around it.

| Area | Measure |
|---|---|
| Sign-in | An authenticator code for administrators, course administrators, lecturers and anyone teaching on a course site; account lockout after failed attempts, by account and by network address; sessions end after 30 minutes idle or 8 hours in all; a change of role signs the person out everywhere |
| Access | Access decided by role and by course membership; a student sees only their own work and released marks; an id outside a person's reach reads as unknown; access review each term, signed off |
| Audit log | Every change, sign-in and download recorded with who, when and from where; the log is insert-only and each entry is chained to the one before by a keyed fingerprint; the chain is checked every night and a break alerts the administrators and the auditor |
| Encryption | Secrets and sensitive identifiers encrypted in the database (authenticator secrets, certificate check codes, private calendar links), with the key held outside the database; masked in the audit log; all traffic over TLS |
| Uploads | Files checked by their content, not their name; Office files only without macros; size limits; stored under random names |
| Browser | Content-Security-Policy; no third-party scripts, analytics or advertising; only the session and form-protection cookies |
| Backups | Daily encrypted backup of the database and the file store, with a copy off site; restore drill timed at 8 hours or less (item 7.09) [status to be confirmed at go-live] |
| Location | Real data kept in Guyana (item 7.04) |
| Environments | Staging (Railway, United States) and development hold fictional data only |
| Retention | Retention schedule as data; disposal approved by a second person and audited; sign-in attempts and old notifications removed every night |
| People | Confidentiality undertakings and training (clause 7); least access for the Provider's staff |
| Software | Locked dependencies checked for known vulnerabilities and licences on every change; secret scan; review of every change before it reaches production |
| Monitoring | Alerts on failures and on the audit chain check (item 7.10) [to be confirmed at go-live] |
